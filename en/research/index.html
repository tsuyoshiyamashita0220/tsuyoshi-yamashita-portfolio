Research philosophy

Measure the distance, find the barrier, cross it with expression.

Expression science is the study of the distances and barriers that exist between people, between people and information, and between people and society — and the design and testing of means of expression that cross them.

This is the research definition used by HYOGEN LAB 408. It is not a definition of “expression science” as an established academic field.

Before splitting people into B2B, B2C or personas, we first look at distance.

But being close does not mean being understood. Sometimes words fail the person right in front of you. Sometimes a single word reaches someone on the other side of the planet.

What makes the difference is the barrier in between. Voice, video, story, silence or experience may be able to cross it. We study, make and test that.

Generation · Reception · ReturnBorn, crossing, returning

Expression is born inside you, crosses barriers to reach others, and comes back to you again. We study this one loop as three streams. The rulers for measuring the middle part, “crossing,” are distance and barriers, below.

  1. AGENERATIONBornHow is expression born?How experiences, emotions, thoughts and physical states become voice, words and works. We study what happens before expression comes out.
  2. BRECEPTIONCrossingWhy does the same expression reach people differently?Meaning changes with the receiver's knowledge, expectations, mood and context. We measure the distances and barriers on the way.Measure with distance and barriers ↓
  3. CRETURNReturningWhat happens when you receive your own expression?Listening again to your own voice, writing and story as a receiver. We study how perception, emotion and meaning change at that moment.

↺ From what comes back, the next expression is born

Voice, words, pauses, body, story — we study things that seem separate as a single flow.

Research questionsResearch questions

  1. Why does skillful expression sometimes fail to get across?
  2. Why do the same words mean different things to different people?
  3. To what extent does a pause (ma) change the receiver's interpretation?
  4. How do voice, facial expression, body and story work together?
  5. When experience is turned into expression, does our relationship with that experience change?
  6. When you receive again what you created, what changes?

Axis 1 · DistanceAxis 1: Distance

How far the expression is meant to reach (its intended range).

  1. 0 mYourselfFacing your own voice and thoughts
  2. 1 mThe person in front of youTwo people, family, one-on-one lessons
  3. 10 mPeople in the same placeClassrooms, venues, workplaces
  4. 1 kmPeople in the same townHibikino, Kitakyushu
  5. 100 kmPeople elsewhere in the countryJapanese-language streaming, books
  6. Other side of the worldPeople around the worldReaching across languages and borders

Even up close, it may not reach. Even far away, it may.

Axis 2 · BarrierAxis 2: Barriers

The barrier the expression is trying to cross.

  • PHYSICALPhysical barrierToo far, unable to travel, unable to be thereWays to expressVideo, audio, streaming, remote experiences
  • PSYCHOLOGICALPsychological barrierAnxiety, hesitation, wariness, awkwardnessWays to expressWays of speaking, pauses, expression that invites empathy
  • TEMPORALTemporal barrierGaps between eras, times that don't matchWays to expressRecords, stories, archives
  • LANGUAGE & CULTURELanguage & culture barrierDifferences in language, values and customsWays to expressTranslation, visual expression, cultural adaptation
  • COGNITIVECognitive barrierInvisible, hard to understand, too specializedWays to expressMetaphors, diagrams, experiential works
  • SOCIAL & INSTITUTIONALSocial & institutional barrierGaps created by position, affiliation and rulesWays to expressSpaces for dialogue, ways to take part, designing what is made public

Removing every barrier is not the answer. Some barriers are necessary — privacy, safety, confidentiality. What we study is not how to break barriers, but how to tell which ones should be crossed, and how to make contact through fitting expression.

Popularity scienceThe science of popularity

We look at how far an expression that crossed a barrier has traveled inside people, using four stages and seven indicators. “Popularity” is the result of these seven connecting. Measuring and testing that process is what we call “the science of popularity.”

  1. Reach
    Interest
    Did they notice it? Did they watch or listen to the end?
    Understanding
    Can they explain it in their own words?
  2. Move
    Empathy
    Were they moved?
    Trust
    Did they feel they could believe in it or rely on it?
  3. Stay
    Memory
    Can they recall it after time has passed?
  4. Spread
    Action
    Did they actually act — apply, visit, buy?
    Sharing
    Did they tell or recommend it to others?

The order is a guide; people may move back and forth. The measures are examples.
